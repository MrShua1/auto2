# Story Design For AI Shorts

## Purpose

Compress a narrative into a duration the video workflow can actually generate. This layer owns dramatic causality, not camera prompts.

## Brief Interview

Resolve only unknowns that change the production:

1. Target duration and aspect ratio.
2. Audience/platform and language.
3. Original story, licensed adaptation, parody, or theme-only inspiration.
4. Desired ending emotion and the protagonist's final choice.
5. Target generator/surface, if known.
6. Available character/location references.
7. Budget or maximum generation attempts.

If the user does not know a setting, choose a conservative default and label it. Do not block ideation on optional preferences.

## Story Spine

Write these seven lines before scenes:

1. **Normal:** What stable but incomplete life exists?
2. **Disruption:** What visible event makes normal impossible?
3. **Pursuit:** What does the protagonist now try to achieve?
4. **Escalation:** What repeated action increases cost rather than repeating information?
5. **Reversal:** What new truth or failed tactic changes the pressure?
6. **Choice:** What costly action reveals change?
7. **Afterimage:** What final image proves the new state without explaining it?

Every line must cause the next. Replace coincidence chains with decisions, discoveries, or consequences.

## Five-Minute Scene Budget

Default to 5-8 scenes. A useful starting shape:

| Phase | Approx. time | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Hook / normal | 0-35s | Establish visual rule and absence |
| Disruption | 35-75s | Force a concrete objective |
| Pursuit | 75-165s | Two or three escalating attempts |
| Reversal | 165-220s | Change the meaning or available choice |
| Choice / climax | 220-275s | Protagonist acts and pays a cost |
| Afterimage | 275-300s | Let image, sound, or omission close the film |

These are planning ranges, not a mandatory formula. The final scene durations must sum exactly to the target.

## AI-Generatability Rewrite

For each story beat, ask:

- Can the change be seen or heard in one location and one generation-sized clip?
- Is the action physically legible without requiring precise long dialogue?
- Can identity survive the number of people, props, reflections, crowds, and costume changes?
- Can a cut replace a difficult continuous transformation?
- Does the beat need a new canonical reference image?

Prefer one expressive action over simultaneous action stacks. Prefer editorial implication over technically fragile spectacle.

## Adaptation Boundary

When adapting an existing work:

- Keep themes, emotional geometry, or a broad premise.
- Create original scenes, dialogue, visual motifs, and ending construction.
- Do not reproduce iconic shots, dialogue, score cues, or scene order unless rights are confirmed.
- Mark the project as `theme-inspired`, `licensed-adaptation`, or `original` in the brief.

## Story Gate

Pass G1 only when:

- The protagonist has a visible objective.
- At least three scene changes are causal, not merely chronological.
- The reversal changes pressure or meaning.
- The climax is a choice, not an explanation.
- The final image demonstrates consequence.
- The scene durations sum to target.

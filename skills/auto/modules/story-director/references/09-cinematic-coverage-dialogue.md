# Cinematic Coverage And Dialogue

## Purpose

Turn plot information into an edited scene with spatial depth, point of view, performance, and consequential speech. This layer blocks flat action-state storyboards and decorative inserts.

## Coverage Contract

For each scene or generation clip, choose only the editorial views that perform a distinct job:

1. **Tactile or mechanical detail:** lets the location act before the character appears. Examples: rail vibration, barrier hinge, condensation, envelope edge, bicycle chain.
2. **Spatial establish:** shows where forces, exits, obstacles, and characters are in relation to one another.
3. **Subjective or information view:** shows what a character notices, misses, waits for, or cannot reach.
4. **Performance and reaction:** records a decision, withheld response, gaze change, breath, or physical consequence.
5. **Afterimage:** leaves the changed space, object, or scale after the action; use only when it changes meaning or creates the next transition.

Do not force all five into every clip. A shot survives only when removing it would lose space, pressure, information, relationship, transition, or emotional consequence.

## Scale Progression

Use shot scale as grammar, not decoration:

- Detail before wide can make the audience discover space from touch and sound.
- Wide before close can place the character under environmental pressure.
- Close before extreme wide can turn private emotion into distance or fate.
- Repeating the same scale is valid when matching time states, but not as the default for an entire scene.

Avoid generic sequences such as `wide -> medium -> close` unless each cut changes story information.

## Insert Test

Every insert must answer one question:

- What space are we entering?
- What force is approaching?
- What obstacle is activating?
- What object carries the relationship?
- What has changed in time?
- What choice or failure will this image cause?
- What visual or sound match carries us into the next shot?

Delete inserts that merely look attractive.

## Dialogue Function

Every spoken line must do at least one job:

- Pursue something from the other person.
- Avoid or redirect a difficult subject.
- Reveal that two people interpret the same event differently.
- Make a promise whose later cost can be seen.
- Ask a question that changes the next action.
- Leave a non-answer that becomes an edit or story beat.

Do not let dialogue describe visible action, announce emotion, summarize backstory already shown, or imitate a protected screenplay.

## Dialogue Shape

Prefer short exchanges with pressure and subtext:

```text
Character A: concrete observation or request.
Character B: answer that reveals a different need.
Character A: adjustment, refusal, promise, or unfinished line.
Visible consequence: hand stops, door closes, gaze shifts, object is kept, person leaves.
```

The strongest final line often creates an unanswered physical action rather than explaining the theme.

## Lip-Sync Design

- Put sync dialogue in locked or gently moving close/medium shots.
- One active speaker per shot is preferred.
- Keep head turns, hand choreography, weather effects across the mouth, and camera movement minimal while a line is spoken.
- Use reaction shots for the listener instead of making both mouths move in one fragile frame.
- Put long narration, letters, announcements, and phone voices off screen or produce them in post.
- Record ambience and score separately unless native generation is explicitly required.

## Sound-First Editing

Use J-cuts and L-cuts to make locations continuous:

- Let a warning bell, train, wind, rocket, elevator, or room tone enter before its source appears.
- Carry one sound across a hard visual cut when it reveals causal connection.
- Remove score before decisive dialogue or environmental impact.
- Let silence or reduced ambience mark intimacy; restore ordinary sound when the scene returns to reality.

## Identity And Frame Masters

- Every storyboard shot is first generated as one independent 16:9 frame.
- Character-bearing frames must include the approved original identity reference on the actual generation request.
- Original identity controls facial geometry, features, hairline, hairstyle, age presentation, and body proportions.
- Composition references control location, framing, blocking, wardrobe, and props only.
- A visibly different face is a failed take even when hashes prove the identity file was sent.
- Build contact sheets only after frame approval. Preserve the complete 16:9 frame, scale proportionally, and use neutral whitespace when required.

## Gate

Block the shotboard when:

- A clip is only setup/action/end states from one angle with no editorial reason.
- Dialogue does not change pressure or relationship.
- Complex movement and lip-sync compete in one shot.
- Inserts are decorative rather than causal.
- The environment exists only as a background behind characters.
- Independent frames are not 16:9.
- Identity is accepted from request metadata without visual comparison.

# Cinematic LOOK Routing For Storyboard Frames

## Boundary

Storyboard state and continuity are immutable inputs. A cinematic LOOK can
change rendering behavior, not who or what appears in the frame.

The separately installed `cinematic-prompt-engine` Skill by Leo414x may be used
for personal/non-commercial still-image prompt work under its own restricted
`LICENSE`. Preserve its attribution and do not treat it as part of this Skill's
license. Commercial use may require written authorization from its author.

## Priority Order

1. Frozen panel state and narrative job.
2. Original identity references.
3. Written per-panel wardrobe contract and forbidden source garments.
4. Blocking, screen direction, props, location and weather.
5. Project LOOK lock.
6. Model adapter and endpoint settings.

LOOK never overrides levels 1-4.

## Compile The LOOK

Record one stable project `look_lock_id`. For each panel, append only the
photography layers needed to realize that lock:

- scene-aware visual center
- framing and angle already selected by the storyboard
- exactly one motivated depth mechanism
- camera/lens response
- motivated source, direction, fill and falloff
- palette, skin anchor, tonal density and restrained texture
- subject-aware realism and optical clarity
- one genre-appropriate, model-aware anti-slop treatment

Do not use studio names or `cinematic` as substitutes for these observable
choices. Do not imply official affiliation with a named studio, show or DP.

## Continuity Gate

Across panels, keep camera response, lens family, skin rendering, tonal curve,
texture scale and realism stable. Permit scene color-temperature changes only
when time, location, weather or practical light motivates them.

Reject a panel when the LOOK causes:

- identity, age or hairstyle drift
- source-clothing leakage or wardrobe drift
- body-count, hand, foot or prop duplication
- unmotivated fog, particles or volumetric shafts
- waxy skin, beauty filtering or digital over-sharpening
- smeared background structure
- unrelated global color wash
- arbitrary lens-family or grain-scale changes
